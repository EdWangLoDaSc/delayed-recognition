How is Beauty score B computed?

B (Van Raan, 2004) measures how much a paper's early citation history dipped below a straight line connecting its publication year to its peak-citation year. Higher B = longer, deeper dormancy before a sharp awakening.

B = Σ over t = 0…t_peak of (ℓ(t) − c(t)) / max(1, ℓ(t)) where ℓ(t) = c(0) + (c(t_peak) − c(0)) · t / t_peak c(t) = citations received in year t after publication t_peak = year index of the citation peak
  • ℓ(t) — the "expected" linear growth baseline from year 0 to the peak.
  • ℓ(t) − c(t) — how far the paper fell below that baseline in year t.
  • Each year's gap is normalized by the baseline, then summed up to the peak.
  • If the peak comes within the first 2 years (t_peak ≤ 1), B is forced to 0 — instant hits do not count as sleeping beauties.

Reading B: B ≈ 0 → smooth growth, no dormancy. Larger B → the paper was systematically under-cited for years before its eventual awakening.

Data contract and cautions

Methods

The analytical object is an OpenAlex work with a DOI, a primary field assignment, yearly citation bins, and a local delay score. The corpus is a high-citation, stratified sample for interaction and hypothesis formation, not a neutral census of all papers in each discipline.
